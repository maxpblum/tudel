● The Batch 3 first draft (unit U12, "Form, arc & zen") is in output/batch3/. It

  The skills and how they connect
  - zen.section-arrange: parts are named and grouped into named sections (built
    with stack), and a single arrange line at the bottom gives the form, like
    the road map on a band chart.
  - zen.loop-layering: seqPLoop written as a conductor's cue sheet, one row per
    part, for staggered entrances. The exercises include a build-up and a
    chorale whose voices enter from the bass up.
  - zen.dramatic-arc: six things that raise or lower intensity: density,
    register, brightness, loudness, harmony and key. Sections act like terraced
    dynamics and signals like hairpins. The main example is a 20-bar piece whose
    breakdown is a dominant pedal over a cadential six-four, set up like a
    retransition in sonata form. Also covered: a noise riser, the semitone lift,
    and Boléro's late key change.
  - zen.readability-refactor: five signs of sprawling code, each with its fix,
    and one piece refactored step by step. The code is laid out like a score:
    tempo, then parts, then sections, then the form.
  - zen.consolidated-idioms: the course's idiom notes reduced to nine
    principles, a 16-bar example piece, and a self-review checklist.

  The exercises cover all four types the brief asked for (arrange, refactor,
  read-the-code, creative), plus recall, transform, sweep and spec-to-code. The
  final creative variant is a "graduation piece" in the learner's choice of
  genre, with a chiptune reference answer.

  Two Strudel behaviours the lessons are built on. I checked both in the pinned
  source and by running them; STRUDEL_API_M3.md gets them wrong or leaves them
  out:
  - Different clocks: arrange gives each section its own clock, so a section
    starts on its own first step. seqPLoop uses the song's bar count, so a part
    can come in partway through its pattern. The lessons contrast the two
    directly, and this is why a 4-bar riser inside a 4-bar section always starts
    from the bottom.
  - Loop length: seqPLoop's loop length is the end of the last entry you write,
    not the largest end. Writing the entries out of order can make a part
    disappear entirely.
